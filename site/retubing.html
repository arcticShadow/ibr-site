Retubing

A new tube set, on the hull you already own.

Retubing gives you the look, feel and pressure of a new inflatable without buying one. The hull, motor and trailer you already trust stay exactly where they are.

Hypalon, PVC or TPU

This one decision drives most of the cost and nearly all of the lifespan.

Average service life varies with storage, UV exposure and use. A boat kept on a mooring in full sun works far harder than a tender under cover.
Material Average life Strengths Best suited to
Hypalon ~30 years Excellent UV and dry abrasion resistance; repairable almost indefinitely; glued seams Boats you intend to keep; moorings and open storage; commercial and rescue use
PVC ~7 years Lower cost; fast to fabricate; can be welded rather than glued Covered storage, lighter use, tenders, and shorter ownership horizons
TPU Long, with high abrasion resistance Very tough for its weight; welded seams; strong against scuffing Hard-working commercial and rescue craft that take physical punishment

Hypalon is properly called chlorosulfonated polyethylene. It's chosen for longevity and dry abrasion resistance, and it is the material most often specified where a boat has to last. PVC is chosen mainly for speed of production and a lower price at the counter.

The honest comparison isn't the invoice, it's the invoice divided by the years. A PVC set at a lower price that needs replacing in seven years is not cheaper than a Hypalon set that runs for thirty. It is only cheaper if you're selling the boat inside the decade — which, for plenty of owners, is exactly the plan. Tell us the plan and we'll tell you which one makes sense.

What a retube involves

1. Assessment

We look at the hull, transom, floor and fittings, not just the tubes. There is no point building a new tube set onto a transom that is about to let go.

2. Pattern and build

Tubes are patterned to your hull, so the new set fits the boat you actually have rather than the one that left the factory. Cones, chambers, rubbing strake, grab lines, handles and D-rings are all specified at this point.

3. Strip and prepare

The old set comes off and the hull bonding surface is cleaned back properly. Most retube failures elsewhere trace back to this step being rushed.

4. Fit, pressure test, finish

The set is bonded on, inflated, and left under pressure so any weep shows itself here rather than at the ramp. Fittings and valves go back on last.

While the boat is in

A retube is the one time the boat is stripped, accessible and off the water anyway. It is much cheaper to deal with a soft floor, a tired transom, a hull repair, an outboard service or a trailer warrant in the same visit than to book the boat in twice.

Timing

Book it in the off-season

New Zealand's season runs from Labour Weekend to Easter, and the workshop fills accordingly. From September onwards a large share of the bench is taken by boats that need to be on the water in weeks.

A retube is a planned job, not an emergency one. Booked between autumn and late winter it moves through without competing for space against the pre-season rush — which is better for your turnaround and better for the work.

For commercial and charter operators this matters more than price. Tell us your off-water window and we will schedule the retube into it.

Get a retube quote

Ring us with the make, length and material and we can usually give you a sensible range on the phone. For a firm number we need to see the boat — or good photos of it.
