Custom & manufacturing

If it holds air and nobody makes it, we probably can.

Alongside repairs, this workshop designs and builds one-off inflatable products — for boat clubs, rescue services, researchers and people who turned up with a sketch.

Discuss a build Send a drawing

What we make

Marker buoys & fenders

Made to order in PVC or Hypalon, any size, and repaired as well as built. Boat clubs are our biggest customers for these — race marks live a hard life.

Replacement tube sets

Tube replacement in both PVC and Hypalon, patterned to the hull rather than pulled off a shelf.

Vulcanised rubber

Rubber inner tube repair or replacement, using the hot vulcanising machine in the workshop — a capability very few places still have.

Hovercraft skirts

Skirts for the leisure market, and directional air fingers produced for the Auckland Airport rescue hovercraft.

One-off design & build

Bring the problem rather than the specification. Much of what we have built started as a conversation about something that didn't exist yet.

Specialist repair

White water rafts, dry bags, marker buoys, helicopter float bags and surf life saving craft — the work that needs someone who has seen it before.

Development work

Projects that didn't exist until someone asked

Over the years we've been brought in on development work well outside conventional boat building.

Project Jonah whale rescue pontoons

We worked with Project Jonah to develop a set of pontoons for refloating stranded or injured whales and carrying them back out to sea. It was a world first, and we still manufacture these sets and send them around the world.

And a longer tail of odd ones

  • Rehabilitation shoes for horses
  • A helicopter flight simulator
  • Hovercraft skirts and directional air fingers
  • Inflatable marine mammals for school and university teaching
  • A dolphin replica built for the marine department at Taiwan University
  • A banana boat for the Sheraton in Fiji

If you have something in this territory, get in touch and describe it.

Got something unusual?

A sketch on the back of an envelope is a perfectly good starting point. Call and describe it, or send the drawing and the dimensions.
